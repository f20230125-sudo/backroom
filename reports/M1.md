SPEC conflicts and implementation choices:

- The approximate 25-email target conflicts with the mandatory >=25% document-share gate: 90 standalone invoice PDFs and 84 payout statements already make 174 non-email documents. With 25 emails the share would be only 12.56%. I asked which requirement should take precedence, then used the recommended 60-email volume to satisfy the explicit gate when no response arrived. Attachment and held-out E2 counts remain 10 and five; the extra volume is E1 body invoices. This is a declared deviation from the approximate email volume.
- The 92 dates intersect 14 Monday-Sunday weeks, rather than exactly 13: 29 June-5 July through 28 September-4 October. Each statement includes only reporting-window POS days. This gives 84 statements, 552 payout day-lines, and following-Wednesday bank receipts from 8 July through 7 October. No sales outside 1 July-30 September are invented.
- `documents[]` is the classifier corpus using the six kinds specified in SPEC 1.5. POS/ledger CSVs are explicitly inventoried in `feeds[]`; all physical generated files are covered by the union. Email share uses that classifier-document denominator. Invoice attachments carry a full InvoiceRecord on the containing email; duplicate attachment links identify the standalone original.
- Master emirate codes use DU/SH as requested by SPEC 1.1. The read-only e-invoicing reference uses DXB/SHJ, so M3 needs an explicit mapping rather than treating DU as a new unplanted master-data error. Invoice VAT rates use percentage points (5); platform rates use fractions (0.25, 0.22, 0.28). Credit-note quantities and amounts are signed negative.

Limits and remaining uncertainty: no unresolved M1 gate failures. L3 uses the installed C:/Windows/Fonts/tahoma.ttf without copying or downloading a font.
